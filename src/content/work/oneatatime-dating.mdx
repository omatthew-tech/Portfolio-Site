## The Story

OneAtATime Dating is an introvert-centered iOS dating app. Instead of optimizing for endless swipes, stacked matches, and shallow first impressions, the product focuses on helping people get to know one potential match at a time.

Traditional dating apps often ask introverted users to keep up with multiple conversations, guess whether someone is actually interested, and make quick judgments from a few photos. The product question became: what would dating feel like if the interface protected attention instead of draining it?

## Research Signals

I surveyed nine potential users and launched a landing page to gauge whether the idea felt different enough from Tinder, Bumble, and Hinge. The strongest signal was not that people wanted another dating app. It was that they wanted a different pace and a clearer reason to believe the experience would not become another loop of surface-level browsing.

## Design Iteration

I explored multiple Figma prototypes before building the product: profile-card layouts, expanded profile views, match states, and different ways to reveal more information over time. The early concepts validated the core direction, but they also showed that the interaction model needed to make the product difference obvious in the first few seconds.

## The Solution

The product uses a one-at-a-time matching flow where users progressively reveal more profile information, prompts, and photos. "Likes" became "Reveals," shifting the interaction away from instant approval and toward curiosity.

## Key Product Decisions

- Chatting with matches reveals more profile information, prompts, and photos, giving users a reason to keep the conversation going.
- The interest timer helps users understand whether someone is engaged without relying on ambiguous silence.
- The landing page was refined to explain the product difference sooner and show the app experience more clearly.
- The sign-up flow was shortened, reducing incomplete signups and helping more users reach the active product.
- Users can reach the dating page before answering every profile question, then complete a few more prompts when they are ready to match.

## Outcome

The result was a product experience with a clearer point of view: fewer simultaneous matches, more intentional reveals, and better signals around interest. The research and landing-page tests also gave me a sharper explanation of the product: not "another dating app for introverts," but a calmer way to discover one person at a time.

This project later became the starting point for Test4Test, because building OneAtATime made the need for faster, reciprocal user feedback painfully clear.
